Introduction

Fast fashion is a term used to describe the rapid production of inexpensive clothing in response to the latest trends. While it allows consumers to access trendy clothing at low prices, it has significant environmental impacts.

Environmental Impact

The fast fashion industry contributes to climate change through various means, including:

  • High carbon emissions from production and transportation.
  • Water pollution from dyeing processes.
  • Waste generation due to the short lifespan of garments.

More Information

Clothes in landfills can produce methane When clothing is dumped in landfills, natural fibers such as cotton and wool can decompose. In the low-oxygen environment of a landfill, decomposition can produce methane, a powerful greenhouse gas. Synthetic clothes may not decompose easily, but they can remain in landfills for very long periods. Methane affects climate change because it is a greenhouse gas. When methane is released into the atmosphere, it traps heat from the Earth, making the planet warmer. In simple terms: Clothing waste → clothes decompose in landfills → methane is released → methane traps heat → Earth gets warmer → climate change. Methane is especially concerning because it is much more powerful at trapping heat than CO₂, even though it doesn't stay in the atmosphere as long. Conclusion

To mitigate the environmental impact of fast fashion, consumers can opt for sustainable fashion choices, support ethical brands, and reduce overall consumption.
