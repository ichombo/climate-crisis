About Us

At Thread Cycle, we believe clothes should have more than one life. We are a nonprofit focused on raising awareness about clothing waste and its impact on our planet. We encourage people to reuse, donate, and rethink what they wear so that fewer clothes end up as waste.

Together, we can keep the cycle going.

Our Mission

Our mission is to reduce clothing waste by inspiring people to reuse, donate, and make more thoughtful choices about what they wear.

Through education and community action, we want to make sustainable clothing choices easier and more accessible for everyone.

Our Vision

We envision a future where clothing stays in use instead of ending up in landfills.

By keeping clothes in the cycle for longer, we can reduce waste, conserve resources, and create a more sustainable future.

Environmental Impact

The fast fashion industry contributes to climate change through various means, including:

  • High carbon emissions from production and transportation.
  • Water pollution from dyeing processes.
  • Waste generation due to the short lifespan of garments.

More Information

Fast fashion is a business model built on producing large volumes of cheap clothing very quickly, following trends that change from week to week. Because the clothes are inexpensive and designed to be replaced, many garments are worn only a handful of times before they are thrown away.

The United Nations Environment Programme estimates that the fashion industry is responsible for between 2 and 8 percent of global greenhouse gas emissions. Most of these emissions come from making fabrics, especially synthetic fibers like polyester, which are made from fossil fuels.

Water is another hidden cost. Producing a single cotton t-shirt can take around 2,700 liters of water, about what one person drinks in two and a half years. Textile dyeing and finishing are also among the largest sources of industrial water pollution in the world.

At the end of a garment's life, less than 1 percent of the material used to make clothing is recycled into new clothing. The rest is burned, buried in landfills, or shipped overseas, where it often becomes waste in countries that did not create it.

Every item that is repaired, swapped, resold, or donated is one less item that needs to be made from scratch. Small habits add up when millions of people take part.

Conclusion

To mitigate the environmental impact of fast fashion, consumers can opt for sustainable fashion choices, support ethical brands, and reduce overall consumption.
