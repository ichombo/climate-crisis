Fast Fashion News

Stay informed about how the clothing industry affects our climate, and what people around the world are doing to change it.

Europe Moves to Make Brands Pay for Textile Waste

The European Union has agreed on new rules that will require clothing brands to help pay for the collection, sorting, and recycling of the textiles they sell. Under this approach, known as extended producer responsibility, companies that produce more waste will pay more. EU countries have also been required to collect used textiles separately from regular trash since January 2025.

France Takes Aim at Ultra Fast Fashion

Lawmakers in France have advanced legislation targeting ultra fast fashion companies that release thousands of new styles every day. The proposal includes environmental penalties on low cost, high volume clothing and limits on advertising for these brands, with the goal of slowing down overproduction.

Mountains of Used Clothes in the Atacama Desert

In northern Chile, huge piles of unsold and discarded clothing have built up in the Atacama Desert. Much of it was shipped from other countries as secondhand clothing but could not be resold. Synthetic fabrics in these piles can take hundreds of years to break down, and fires at the dumps release toxic smoke into nearby communities.

Secondhand Clothing Keeps Growing

Thrift stores, resale apps, and clothing swaps are more popular than ever, especially among young people. Buying used clothing extends the life of each garment and reduces the demand for new production, which is where most of fashion's climate impact comes from.

Microplastics From Our Closets

Synthetic fabrics like polyester, nylon, and acrylic shed tiny plastic fibers every time they are washed. These microfibers pass through water treatment plants and end up in rivers and oceans. Washing clothes less often, using cold water, and choosing natural fibers can help reduce this pollution.

What You Can Do

  • Buy less and choose pieces you will wear for years.
  • Shop secondhand or swap clothes with friends.
  • Repair clothes instead of replacing them.
  • Donate or resell clothing you no longer wear.
  • Support brands that are open about how and where their clothes are made.
